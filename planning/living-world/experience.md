# Experience and art direction

## Creative thesis

A miniature coastal settlement becomes recognizably yours through the things you
actually do. Small actions make small, specific changes. Your history forms the
surrounding landscape; zooming out reveals patterns without judging your life.
The reward is recognition, agency and curiosity, rather than pressure to log more.

The emotional sequence is **arrive → notice → act → see a consequence → choose →
remember**. A visit may stop after any step. The useful action should be available
before any decorative performance finishes. A quiet visit must still feel welcome.

## Beautiful: a place, not a dashboard background

Use the suite's warm paper/ink/teal for interface chrome. The scene has a distinct
coastal palette: sea-glass water, chalk paths, warm timber, moss, dusk windows.
Keep the existing crisp sprite idiom and local SVG assets; establish a common
pixel grid, ground baseline, sprite scale and silhouette vocabulary before adding
assets. Do not mix glossy 3D, unrelated illustration styles and pixel silhouettes.
Scene assets can have richer color; text, focus and controls retain shared tokens.

Build depth in three restrained planes: sky/sea, inhabited ground, foreground
plants/path. Architecture should reveal purpose at small size: library books and
warm reading window; inn luggage/sign; garden trellis and flowers; forge chimney.
A building's stage changes its silhouette, not merely its tint. Avoid constant
sparkles, floating numeric badges, generic card grids and oversized empty heroes.

PLACE-01 delivers actual local vector studies and rendered static compositions,
not just adjectives: founded/unfounded, first garden, mature town, current week,
selected past moment, empty/partial Pixels, in light/dark at 390×844 and 1280×900.
Include a 320px stress view and 200% text zoom. A seeded garden study is the
visual target used by PLACE-04. References and asset licenses must be recorded;
no copied commercial characters, logos or sound effects.

## Interactive: verbs with understandable results

| Scale | Main verbs | Immediate response | Useful information |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Town | tend, inspect, arrange | affected lot responds; pending and committed differ | habit status, next change, source availability |
| World | move, inspect, revisit | object highlights; matching dated panel opens | source, date/span, factual explanation, source action |
| Pixels | scan, select, compare, visit | selected day/week stays anchored | counts, composition, coverage, actual date range |
| Hall | understand, preview, reflect | live cosmetic preview; honest save state | authoritative rules, appearance scope, private review |

Interaction always works by touch and keyboard. Hover may preview an affordance
but cannot contain the only explanation. Map panning must not accidentally open
an object. Decorative residents may move; interactive targets stay predictable.
Every animated result has a meaningful static equivalent.

## Informative: tell me why this is here

Each meaningful object answers: what is it, why is it here, when did it happen,
and what can I do next? For example, “Library · Stage 2 · From recorded Media
activity” leads to actual qualifying activity and canonical growth rules. A media
keepsake identifies the completed item and completion date. A travel souvenir
shows the actual trip dates and destination, without pretending the generated
biome is a geographic reconstruction. A learning beacon says which concept was
marked understood and when; it does not certify mastery.

Use qualitative copy only when facts support it. “Three walks recorded this week”
is better than “You're becoming unstoppable.” Show numeric values and date spans
alongside charts and visual metaphors. A changed source updates the representation.
Unknown or unavailable facts stay unknown. Do not label device time as server time.

## Signature moments

1. **First garden.** Choose a first habit, see a modest garden established, and
   find the action to tend it immediately. No long tutorial or forced naming form.
2. **A promise kept.** A confirmed check-in opens a flower or lights a window at
   the relevant lot. A concise label explains the change; Undo is immediate.
3. **Something to place.** At an existing milestone, choose one of three modest
   ornaments. Preview its place and save. It reflects taste, not rarity or power.
4. **A book comes home.** A qualifying completed book yields a library keepsake
   linked to that item, and the same dated occurrence can be found in World.
5. **A journey remembered.** A completed trip has an identifiable souvenir and
   its recorded span in the atlas. Planned travel remains visibly planned.
6. **Perspective.** Select a day in Pixels, read the actual facts, visit its
   landscape, and return to the same day, view and scroll context.
7. **Welcome back.** After a long absence the place remains intact. Show changes
   since the last visit only when locally known; do not invent a narrative.

## Progression and residents

Keep current Town XP, cadence, caps and lot unlock levels. Do not create another
progression scale. Milestone ornaments are bounded cosmetic options tied to
existing levels/building stages, with one deterministic entitlement identity per
milestone. Provide preview of the next physical change beside its real threshold.
No maximum-level dead end: arranging, remembering and revisiting remain useful.

Residents are a small authored cast built from existing Guide/VivekBot/Terrarian/
Manager identities, after a visual/copy coherence review. Give each one a purpose:
orientation, library memories, habit care, or explaining records. Use short local
fact-conditioned vignettes, not conversational AI or invented personal details.
No resident needs affection points, appointments or daily gifts. An optional
ambient scene is not a new task list.

## Sound, motion and seasons

Core feedback takes roughly 150–450ms and never blocks input. Reserve longer
vignettes for explicit inspection; allow dismissal. Optional sound is off by
default until a deliberate user gesture and choice; mute is obvious and persists
per owner/device. No autoplay music. Use an original tiny sound vocabulary if it
materially improves feedback; absence of sound cannot hide a state change.

Season is cosmetic. Reuse TOWN-05's Default/Spring/Summer/Autumn/Winter/automatic
setting. Automatic follows its existing documented browser-local month mapping,
not inferred location, climate or hemisphere. Explicit choice wins. Time-of-day
ambience must be labelled presentation and must not move the authoritative day.

## Visual review rubric

For every representative screen, record a reasoned assessment of composition,
legibility, silhouette recognition, selected-state clarity, purposeful detail,
and consistency between scales. Each must pass with evidence and an actionable
resolution for defects. A screenshot diff can catch change; it cannot establish
beauty. A green functional suite alone cannot close the art-direction requirement.
